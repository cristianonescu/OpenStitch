# OpenStitch 0.1.0 alpha 14

The fourteenth alpha of OpenStitch, an independent, cross-platform embroidery design application
for macOS, Windows and Linux, built to work with Brother machines and with designs made in
PE-DESIGN 11. OpenStitch is not affiliated with Brother Industries.

Alpha 14 is a small alpha after the large alpha 13. Most of it brings the stitches closer to
what PE-DESIGN 11.50 sews. Its main changes:
- **Ties and locks as PE-DESIGN sews them:** every letter piece ties in, locks come back exactly
  to their point, and two objects of one thread that touch are joined without a knot.
- **Satin letters that start where PE-DESIGN starts them:** O, S, 8, A, e, M and N.
- **Zigzag net fills that turn with their Direction.**
- **PES 0110 files keep more:** groups, stamps, motif sizes and vertical text.
- **Editor:** Find objects in the Sewing Order list, the smallest hoop that fits, Fit to hoop,
  crash reports, and a Realistic view without light stripes.
- **Check Design** finds thread ends without a tie, tiny sections and too many short stitches.

Some defaults changed, so a design made the same way can sew differently; see "Changed output"
below. **It is still an alpha for testing, not for production work.** Read "Before you sew" below.

### Satin and lettering

- **Satin letters start where PE-DESIGN starts them:**
  - O, S, 8, A and e start on the edge of the stroke nearest the letter's top-left, with
    PE-DESIGN's one-step tie-in and a short run under the satin to come. They started
    elsewhere: O at its bottom, S at its far end, 8 at its waist, A at its apex (#1017)
  - M and N start at the top of the first stem and run down under it before its satin is sewn
    from the foot up; they started at the foot (#940 part)

  Against PE-DESIGN's reference letters, O sews 342 stitches (PE-DESIGN 358), S 274 (278),
  8 366 (373), M 366 (374) and N 291 (293).
- **A's bar ends along the legs,** as PE-DESIGN's does: its end crossings lie at 109.5° and 70.5°
  (PE-DESIGN 110° and 70°), and the left leg is cut at the bar's corners (#938 part).
- **Every letter piece ties in.** A piece that began on two points almost on top of each other
  (Pacifico's i) started untied; its tie-in now steps 0.3 mm out and back along the first real
  stitch (#1032).
- **A stitch in place before a short tie-in,** on a Hoop Size page, as PE-DESIGN 11.50 sews it
  (#1013). See "Changed output".
- **Vertical text** is laid out as PE-DESIGN's: the letters stand one text size apart and run
  down from the click. "ABC" at 8 mm is 24.4 mm tall, PE-DESIGN's height (it was 35.8 mm)
  (#1027).
- **Text Transform, slant 9** is fitted to PE-DESIGN's letter corners, as slant 10 already was
  (#886).

### Fills and lines

- **Zigzag net turns with its Direction,** as PE-DESIGN 11.50's does; it always sewed on the page
  axes. Its bands lie where PE-DESIGN's do at every Direction (within 0.05 mm), and bands that
  reach in from outside the shape are sewn out to its edge (#1043).
- **A switch to Net Fill or zigzag net starts at Direction 0,** PE-DESIGN's default for those
  types (#1054, #1043).
- **No knot where two objects of one thread touch.** When the needle sews straight on from one
  object into the next, the first no longer locks its end and the second no longer ties in, as
  in PE-DESIGN 11.50's files. Between two lines, a move longer than 1.25 mm is trimmed; it was
  2.3 mm (#987).
- **Locks that really lock.** A lock or tie-in that came back a few hundredths of a millimetre
  off its point could be lost when the file was written. It now comes back exactly: the kit's
  PE-compatible storybook goes from 76 thread ends without a tie to 1 (#1032).
- **Two controls that changed nothing are gone:** a programmable fill shows Frequency only while
  Base sewing is on, and a stripes line has no Period (#1042, #1049).

### Files

- **PES 0110 (PE-DESIGN 11's own format) keeps more of a design:**
  - **Groups:** a design with several groups is read and written with all of them. The export
    dropped every group when there was more than one (#608).
  - **Stamps:** a region's stamp is written as PE-DESIGN's Convert from Outline writes it, and a
    PE-DESIGN shape that carries a stamp is read with it. Stamps were dropped on export, and
    such a file imported nothing (#608, #959).
  - **Motif lines and fills:** the motif's width, height and spacing, and a motif fill's size,
    are read, written and exported (#608).
  - **Vertical text** is written as one top-aligned column (#1027). Several columns and other
    alignments are still refused, with a message.
  - **Page mode:** opening a PE-DESIGN 11 file takes its Hoop Size or Custom Size page (#1013).
- **The pattern block in a PES 0110 file is OpenStitch's own:** one plain line, which
  PE-DESIGN 11.50 opens and edits. See "Repository clean-up" below (#1060).
- **SHV preview picture:** its size is PE-DESIGN's, and each colour takes the preview value
  PE-DESIGN uses for the 12 chart colours measured so far. The stitches in the file don't
  change (#989).

### Editor

- **Find objects in the Sewing Order list:** type a name, a stitch type, "text" or a thread
  ("satin", "brother 017") in the field above the list to show only those rows. Enter or
  **Select All Shown** selects them; Esc clears. Ctrl+F opens it while the pointer is over the
  list. Only the list narrows; the design and its order don't change (#745).
- **Hoop Size or Custom Size page:** Design ▸ Design Settings ▸ Page. Picking a hoop gives a
  Hoop Size page, and typing a size a Custom Size page, as in PE-DESIGN's dialog. The choice is
  one undo step and is saved in the `.osd` (#1056).
- **Use smallest hoop that fits** and **Fit to hoop,** one click each in Design Settings. The
  first switches to the smallest hoop of your machine that holds the design and centres it;
  round hoops count by the field they really sew. The second scales the design to fill
  the current hoop, 5 mm from its edge. Shapes and text keep their density; a stitch-only
  object is scaled with a warning that its density changes (#879).
- **Crash reports and Report a Problem:** after an internal error, a short report is kept beside
  the autosave snapshots: the version, your system, the error and the names of the last 20
  commands, with no folder names from your computer. The next start offers **Report a
  Problem**, which is also in the Help menu: it opens a new GitHub issue with the version
  filled in, and **Copy diagnostics** copies the version and system. Reports older than 30 days
  are deleted. Nothing is sent from OpenStitch (#878).
- **Realistic view without moiré:** zoomed-out fills no longer show light stripes, and the view
  draws faster: a 112,000-stitch design takes about 35 ms a frame, less than half the time
  before. If your graphics driver can't do it, the editor falls back to the old drawing by
  itself; the web editor keeps the old drawing (#907, #1020).
- **Machine Setup: needle count** for a multi-needle machine, used by Check Design (#866).

### Check Design

- **Four new checks** (#866):
  - thread ends without a tie, with a fix that sets the object's Tie to "always"
  - sections of fewer than 3 stitches between two thread cuts
  - too many short stitches: more than 160 in 1,000 shorter than 0.3 mm. The highest rate in
    PE-DESIGN 11.50's own reference files is 154.8, in its satin lettering
  - more thread colours than your machine has needles (a note)
- **"Untied" counts only real ties:** a tie or lock counts when it comes back exactly to its
  point (#1032).
- Check Design and the kit's `sew_check` tool read their limits from one table (#866).

### The Assistant

- **Sees and sets more of the editor:** the Sewing Order filter, the page mode, and whether the
  Realistic view draws the new way (#1031, #1056).
- **New commands:** the smallest hoop that fits, Fit to hoop, the page mode, and Report a
  Problem, which gives it the version and system to quote in a bug report (#879, #1056, #878).
- **Its design check** reports the count of notes beside errors and warnings (#1034).
- **Fix:** an editor that is just starting no longer has its scratch folder cleared by another
  editor starting at the same moment (#1001).
- **Benchmark:** new tasks for a towel badge, a machine without a thread cutter, machine stops
  and satin columns per branch (#666, #807, #1006).

### The ready-to-sew kit

- **Previews are placed by millimetres** and drawn with the new Realistic view (render
  version 3). A preview is rewritten only when its pixels change, and each one records the
  render version it was drawn with (#1051, #920).
- **Files regenerated for this alpha's stitching,** among them:
  - 32 files gain the stitch in place before their one-step tie-ins (#1013)
  - `lettering/fonts-and-text.pes` and `lettering/monograms.pes` for the letter starts, A's bar
    and the tie-ins (#1017, #940, #938, #1032)
  - `fills/region-sew-types-2.pes` for the net (#1054, #1043)
  - `wizards/cutwork.pes` and the two Auto Punch storybooks for the joins and locks (#987,
    #1032)
- **The calibration cards:** only the "T1" and "T2" labels and the stitches in place changed,
  not the swatch rows (#291; see "Changed output").
- All 45 kit files pass `sew_check`.

### Under the hood

- **A test that every sewing setting does something:** each setting the Sewing Attributes pane
  offers must change the stitches. The settings that change nothing yet are listed, each with
  its issue (26 when the test was written); the zigzag net's Direction was one of them (#880).
- **A random-clicking test of the whole editor window:** no crash, no hang, and Undo back to the
  start (#880). It found the Paste Attributes freeze listed under "Known issues" (#1084).
- **Private details:** CI fails a pull request that adds a local folder path or a personal
  detail (#1057).
- **Release smoke test:** it now draws a dense fill in the Realistic view on Linux, and runs the
  macOS app from its zip: the demo, a PES file and the same Realistic scene (#1021).
- **Release workflow:** the draft takes only this version's packages; at alpha 13 its checksum
  step failed on the web build's files (#1004).
- CI also checks that the Text Transform presets are what the fitting tool produces from the
  references (#886), and that no committed PES file lists a motif or fill pattern that is not
  the project's own (#1060).

### Repository clean-up

OpenStitch ships none of Brother's patterns, and its repository should hold none either. A
review found that some of the PE-DESIGN files kept as test references did (#1060):

- **Five capture files were removed.** Four listed a PE-DESIGN built-in motif in their header,
  and one used a built-in fill pattern. Three more files that held outlines from PE-DESIGN's
  shape gallery were withdrawn.
- **PE-DESIGN's default pattern was rewritten out of the other captures.** PE-DESIGN writes its
  default decorative fill pattern into every shape it saves. In 332 capture files, all 495
  such blocks were replaced by OpenStitch's placeholder of the same length; every other byte,
  and the stitches, are unchanged.
- **OpenStitch's own PES 0110 files** no longer carry that pattern's layout: the writer puts in
  one plain line of its own.
- **Tests now fail** on any committed PES file with a shape block, motif or fill pattern that is
  not the project's own.
- **Not done:** git history and the earlier tags were not rewritten, so the removed files are
  still in older commits and in the source archives of earlier releases.
- The tests that used the removed files run on new, clean captures where there is one (motif
  lines and motif fill). The programmable fill and shape start point references are still to
  be replaced (#1075).

## Changed output: the same design can sew differently

These defaults changed. Each item says how to get the old behaviour back where there is a way.

- **A stitch in place before a one-step tie-in on a Hoop Size page (#1013):**
  - **What changed:** on PE-DESIGN's Hoop Size page (a 100 × 100 mm page, or a page set by a hoop
    PE-DESIGN knows), each run whose tie-in is one 0.3 mm step out and back now starts with a
    stitch in place where the move lands, as PE-DESIGN 11.50 sews it: one needle point more per
    such run. Satin K at 8 mm: 136 → 137 needle points, and its first stitches now match
    PE-DESIGN's.
  - **Not affected:** runs with a three-step tie-in, designs on a Custom Size page (any other
    page size with no known hoop), an object's own ties (Finish), opened stitch files, and runs
    that already start with six stitches in place (no seventh is added).
  - **Old designs:** an `.osd` saved before this alpha has no page mode stored and opens on a
    Hoop Size page when its page is 100 × 100 mm or its hoop is a known one; its stitches change
    by this stitch in place only.
  - **Old behaviour:** put the design on a Custom Size page: Design ▸ Design Settings ▸ Page ▸
    Custom Size, or `machine.set` `pageMode: "custom"` (`design.new` takes it too, #1056).
- **Vertical text laid out as PE-DESIGN's (#1027):**
  - **What changed:** a vertical text's letters stand one text size apart, running down from the
    click, each capital hanging from the top of its cell, as PE-DESIGN 11.50 lays them out. Before,
    they were stacked by the font's vertical cell (1.2 em) and the column was centred on the
    click. "ABC" at 8 mm: 35.8 mm tall before, 24.4 mm now, PE-DESIGN's own height, and it starts
    at the click instead of above it. Vertical text can now be exported to PES 0110 as one column.
  - **Not affected:** horizontal text, and texts sized by capital height (older files), which keep
    the font's vertical metrics.
  - **Old behaviour:** none; move the text if a saved design relied on the old position.
- **Realistic view drawn without moiré (#1020):**
  - **What changed:** the screen only, not the stitches. The desktop editor's Realistic view
    draws its threads as one mesh with 4× multisampling, so zoomed-out fills no longer show light
    stripes, and it draws faster (a 112,000-stitch design: about 35 ms a frame against 100 ms).
    Dense fills look a little lighter at page fit (4–11 levels; the black ramp up to 22),
    closer to the sewn T1 swatches on white cotton. The ready-to-sew kit's previews were
    redrawn with it (render version 2, #920).
  - **Not affected:** the stitches and every file written; the web editor keeps the old drawing.
  - **Old behaviour:** set the environment variable `OPENSTITCH_NO_MSAA=1` before starting the
    editor (also useful on a remote desktop or a driver that draws multisampling wrongly).
- **Direction now turns a zigzag net (#1043):**
  - **What changed:** a zigzag net region turns by its Direction, as PE-DESIGN 11.50 does; before,
    it always sewed on the page axes. Switching a region to zigzag net now starts it at
    Direction 0, PE-DESIGN's default for the type (a 45° fill switched over used to keep 45°).
    The net starts where PE-DESIGN starts it: from the top-left corner of the shape's bounds
    on the page, half a band in along both turned axes, so its bands lie where PE-DESIGN's do
    at every Direction (within 0.05 mm). As PE-DESIGN does, it also sews the part of a band
    that reaches into the shape from a line running outside it, and where the outline cuts a
    rung and the diagonal back from it, the needle steps along the outline between them. On a
    30 × 20 mm rectangle OpenStitch sews 1,126, 1,088, 840 and 886 stitches at 30°, 45°, 90°
    and 180°, where PE-DESIGN sews 1,148, 1,141, 850 and 923; on 40 × 20 mm at 45° and 90°,
    1,411 and 1,055 against 1,496 and 1,127 (PE-DESIGN also travels along the outline, #1043).
  - **At Direction 0:** a net sews as before except near the outline, where a band reaches in
    from a line outside the shape or the outline cuts a rung and its diagonal back (one needle
    point more on the 30 × 20 mm rectangle). The ready-to-sew kit's
    `fills/region-sew-types-2.pes` (a honeycomb zigzag net) sews its bands out to the edges:
    4,532 → 4,675 stitches.
  - **Not affected:** nets saved by older versions, which load at Direction 0.
  - **Old behaviour:** set the net's Direction to 0 for a net on the page axes; the bands near
    the outline have no setting.
- **A switch to net fill starts at Direction 0 (#1054):**
  - **What changed:** switching a region to Net Fill now sets its Direction to 0, as PE-DESIGN
    11.50 does (a 45° fill switched over used to keep 45°, so its net sewed diagonally). An
    angle given with the switch is kept. The ready-to-sew kit's `fills/region-sew-types-2.pes`
    and the `region-types` example make their net this way, so their net now sews on the page
    axes: the kit file 4,554 → 4,532 stitches, the example's honeycomb net 16,070 → 16,068 for
    the whole design.
  - **Not affected:** nets saved by older versions keep their Direction, since they really
    sewed at it; the Cutwork wizard's net keeps the direction set in the wizard.
  - **Old behaviour:** set the net's Direction to 45 after the switch.
- **Programmable fill: Frequency only with Base sewing (#1042):** the Frequency control is shown
  only while Base sewing is on, as PE-DESIGN greys it otherwise; it staggers the base sewing
  only, so it never changed anything without it. No stitches change.
- **Stripes line: no "Period" control (#1049):** stripes are running rows along the band and
  have no period, so the control, which changed nothing, is gone. No stitches change.

- **The calibration cards are not the files that were sewn (#291):**
  - **What changed:** nothing new for the swatch rows in this alpha. The T1 card was sewn on
    2026-11-03 (main 4cf8b7a) and 2026-11-04 (4cf8b7a or later), but its file has changed since:
    in #299 and substantially in #634 (alpha.12: 4,870 of 5,193 records). In this alpha, #1013
    added only stitches in place before one-step tie-ins, and #938 changed only the left end of
    the "T1" and "T2" labels' T bar (2 runs of records), not the swatch rows. The T2 card and the
    satin and under sewing card have no sew-out yet.
  - **Old behaviour:** `git show 4cf8b7a:examples/sew/calibration/t1-swatches.pes` is the file
    the scans were sewn from.

- **Every letter piece ties in (#1032):**
  - **What changed:** a run that starts on a near-duplicate point (Pacifico's i, a stroke's first
    rung 0.01-0.02 mm from its lead) used to tie in by stepping out that far and back, which the
    writer sews as one needle point, so the piece started untied. The tie-in now steps along the
    first stitch longer than 0.1 mm, PE-DESIGN's 0.3 mm out and back. The kit's fonts-and-text
    had 4 untied letter starts (Pacifico's "ific") and has none; two Auto Punch designs lose a few
    untied starts (storybook 97 to 95, balloon 4 to 3; the rest are Auto Punch ends, #1032).
  - **Not affected:** runs whose first stitch is longer than 0.1 mm.
  - **Old behaviour:** none.

- **Satin letters start where PE-DESIGN starts them (#1017):**
  - **What changed:** a satin letter whose first stroke started away from its box's top-left
    corner now starts as PE-DESIGN's does. That covers O at its bottom, S at its far end, 8 at
    its waist, A at its apex, e lower down, and p and n in the Text Transform 18 capture's
    "OpenStitch". The letter starts on the stroke's edge nearest the top-left with a one-step
    tie-in, runs 1.4–1.9 mm stitches along the stroke under the satin to come, then sews the
    satin back over the run. O's ring is sewn from its top. Counts against PE-DESIGN's 20 mm references:
    - O 338 → 342 (PE 358), S 267 → 274 (278), 8 359 → 366 (373);
    - A 292 → 294 (290) and e 268 → 281 (267). The stitches before the satin now equal
      PE-DESIGN's (7 and 16); the satin after them was already 4 and 14 over (#1080).
    In the kit:
    - `lettering/fonts-and-text.pes`: 3,936 → 3,980 stitches, 40 → 38 trims;
    - `lettering/monograms.pes`: 7,598 → 7,606;
    - `calibration/t2-alternating-rows.pes`: 6,071 → 6,073.
  - **Not affected:** letters whose satin already starts at the stroke end nearest the top-left
    (I, L, H, K, T, V, W, X, Z, i), and M and N (#940's run down the first stem).
  - **Old behaviour:** none.

- **Locks that really lock: a run's lock or tie-in comes back exactly to its point (#1032):**
  - **What changed:** many Auto Punch fill rows end by stepping back and coming home 0.01-0.05 mm
    from where they left. That counted as a lock, so no other was added. But a stitch file stores
    points on a 0.1 mm grid, and two points that close can land on neighbouring grid points: the
    machine then sews no lock there, and the thread can pull out at the trim. Such a lock (or a
    tie-in at a run's start) is now made exact by moving its last point the few hundredths of a mm
    onto the point it comes back to, as PE-DESIGN's are. No stitch is added or removed.
  - **What a sewer notices:** nothing on the fabric beyond the missing locks being there. The kit's
    PE-compatible storybook had 76 thread ends without a tie and has 1 (564 needle points moved,
    0.014 mm on average, at most 0.05 mm). The OpenStitch storybook goes from 5 to 1, the
    PE-compatible balloon from 3 to 0, and gradients from 1 to 0. Stitch counts are unchanged.
  - **Check Design:** its "untied" rule counts a tie or lock only when it comes back exactly. A
    stitch in place at a start counts only when a tie-in follows it, as PE-DESIGN sews on a Hoop
    Size page. PE-DESIGN's own 490 reference files show the same untied ends under the new rule as
    under the old (8).
  - **Not affected:** runs that already end in an exact lock, runs with no lock at all (they get a
    full one, as before), and an object's own ties (Finish).
  - **Old behaviour:** none.

- **SHV preview size as PE-DESIGN's (#989):**
  - **What changed:** the SHV thumbnail is twice the design's half-width in millimetres,
    rounded, plus a pixel each side, and the same for its height, as PE-DESIGN 11.50 writes it.
    A 59.0 or 59.5 mm wide design now gets a 62 px preview where it got 61, and a 69.4 mm one
    72 px where it got 71. The stitch records do not change, and a machine sews the same.
  - **Not affected:** a side whose length in whole millimetres (rounded down) is even keeps its
    size; an odd one grows by a pixel. The pixels are still drawn at one a millimetre:
    PE-DESIGN's own drawing follows no rule we could fit.
  - **Old behaviour:** none.
- **No knot where two objects of one thread touch (#987):**
  - **What changed:** when the needle goes from one object straight on to the next in the same
    thread (a move short enough to be sewn as one stitch, so the thread is not cut), OpenStitch no
    longer sews a small knot at the end of the first object and another at the start of the
    second. On the machine, the thread just runs on from one object into the next, as it does in
    PE-DESIGN 11.50's files. Each such join loses 4 needle points.
  - **Also changed: the line-to-line distance.** Between two objects' lines (outlines), only a
    move of up to 1.25 mm is now sewn across; a longer one is knotted, trimmed and knotted again,
    as PE-DESIGN does. It was 2.3 mm, as between fills.
  - **In the kit:**
    - `wizards/cutwork.pes` gets one trim more, 2 → 3: a 1.95 mm move between two lines is now
      cut.
    - The two Auto Punch storybook designs lose their knots at the joins sewn across:
      `L-autopunch-storybook-openstitch` 95,104 → 95,056 needle points and
      `L-autopunch-storybook-pe-compatible` 114,079 → 113,916. Their trims don't change.
  - **Not affected:** any move into or out of a letter of a text. No PE-DESIGN capture shows
    such a short move, so letters keep their knots until one does (#1082). Also not affected:
    an object's own ties (Finish) and joins that are trimmed.
  - **Old behaviour:** set Finish ▸ Tie to "always" on both objects (#548). The first then
    locks its end and the second ties in at its start, even with no trim between them. No
    setting brings back sewing a 1.25–2.3 mm line-to-line move across.
- **Satin M and N start at the top of the first stem (#940):**
  - **What changed:** M and N start at the top of the left stem and run down under it before its
    satin is sewn from the foot up, as PE-DESIGN's do; they started at the foot. Against
    PE-DESIGN's references: M 358 → 366 (PE 374) and N 283 → 291 (293); at 8 mm, M 148 → 153
    (165) and N 119 → 124 (123). In the kit, `lettering/fonts-and-text.pes` went from 3,876 to
    3,884 needle points and from 39 to 40 trims with this change.
  - **Not affected:** the other satin letters.
  - **Old behaviour:** none.
- **Satin A's bar ends along the legs (#938):**
  - **What changed:** the bar's first and last crossings lie along the legs' inner edges, and
    the left leg is cut through the corners where the bar meets it. Against PE-DESIGN's
    references: A 284 → 292 (PE 290), H at 40 mm 579 → 575 (556), K 314 → 312 (293),
    8 357 → 358 (373) and T 208 → 209 (186). In the kit, `lettering/fonts-and-text.pes` changes
    at its A, H and R.
  - **Old behaviour:** none.
- **Text Transform, slant 9 (#886):** the preset is refitted to PE-DESIGN's letter corners, so a
  text under it moves by tenths of a millimetre: the reference text's right edge went from
  87.4 to 87.6 mm, where PE-DESIGN's is at 87.7. There is no way back to the old fit.
- **SHV preview colours (#989):** each colour block's value in the preview picture is the one
  PE-DESIGN writes for the 12 chart colours measured; another colour takes the nearest measured
  colour's value. The picture only: the stitch records do not change.
- **PES 0110 export, no change to stitches (#608, #1027, #1060):**
  - a design with several groups keeps them, and a region's stamp is written; both were left
    out before
  - motif lines and fills are written with their sizes and spacing
  - a vertical text is written as one column
  - every shape's pattern block is OpenStitch's own one-line pattern

**Re-export designs** made with alpha 13 or earlier if you want the new stitching. A design file
keeps its own settings, and the stitches are made from them when it's exported.

## Before you sew

- **Do a test sew-out on scrap fabric** before using a design on anything that matters.
- **Known issues:**
  - **Paste Attributes can freeze the editor** for over 30 seconds on a large selection that
    was duplicated and grouped (#1084). Save before pasting attributes onto many objects.
  - **Zigzag net:** PE-DESIGN also travels along the outline between bands; OpenStitch does not
    yet, so it sews 1 to 6 % fewer stitches than PE-DESIGN on the rectangles measured (#1043).
  - **Letters keep their lock and tie-in** at a short move sewn across into or out of a letter.
    No PE-DESIGN file shows what it sews there yet (#1082).
  - **Satin e and A** sew 14 and 4 stitches more than PE-DESIGN's after their start (#1080).
  - **Cross Stitch in PE-compatible mode** picks different threads than PE-DESIGN for some colour
    groups (on the pixel strawberry: Dark Fuchsia where PE-DESIGN sews Amber Red, Moss Green for
    its Leaf Green), and leaves a few part-covered edge cells empty that PE-DESIGN sews (#991).
  - **SHV export:** the preview picture has PE-DESIGN's size, but its pixels are drawn by
    OpenStitch's own rule, and Brother colours not yet seen in a PE-DESIGN export take the
    nearest measured colour's value (#989).
  - **Dithered colour blends** cost many trims (#951).
  - **The browser editor** has no Assistant yet (#766), and keeps the old Realistic drawing.
  - Other known differences from PE-DESIGN 11 are in the README's "Known issues" and
    [docs/COMPATIBILITY_AUDIT.md](../COMPATIBILITY_AUDIT.md).
- **Keep your original PE-DESIGN files.** Save OpenStitch's output under a new name.
- **macOS:** double-clicking a file in Finder doesn't open it in OpenStitch yet; use File ▸ Open.
