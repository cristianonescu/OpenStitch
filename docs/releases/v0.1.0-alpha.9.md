# OpenStitch 0.1.0 alpha 9

The ninth alpha of OpenStitch, an independent, cross-platform embroidery design application for
macOS, Windows and Linux, built to work with Brother machines and with designs made in
PE-DESIGN 11. OpenStitch is not affiliated with Brother Industries.

Alpha 9 is about turning images into embroidery. Every image tool (Auto Punch, Photo Stitch 1
and 2, Cross Stitch and Design Center) now has an **OpenStitch mode**, measured against the
source image with a likeness score, and threads can come from other brands' charts or your own.
The default stays the **PE-DESIGN-compatible mode**: OpenStitch's own reimplementation of
PE-DESIGN 11's method, unchanged and bit-identical to alpha 8. Neither mode's pictures are
PE-DESIGN's output. **It is still an alpha for testing, not for production work.** Read "Before
you sew" below.

## What changed since alpha 8

### Image digitizing: OpenStitch mode for every image tool ➕

Choose it in the image dialog's **Mode** row. Each tool keeps its PE-DESIGN-compatible mode as the
default.

- **Likeness, measured:** a score from 0 to 100 compares the sewn result, rendered at actual size,
  with the source image: 40 % structure (MS-SSIM), 40 % colour (CIEDE2000) and 20 % texture
  (DISTS). `tools/image_similarity.py` computes it, and the README gallery shows it for both
  modes.
- **Needle density:** OpenStitch mode stays within limits a machine sews well. On average it
  uses at most 2.1 needle points per mm², and no 10 × 10 mm area goes above 3.0. Photo Stitch 2
  may use up to 3.3, PE-DESIGN's own level, to keep its line look. In Auto Punch a normal
  single-layer fill or satin always counts as within the limits.
- **Thread sources:** Brother's chart, Madeira Polyneon, Isacord 40, Sulky Rayon 40, Robison-Anton
  Rayon and Polyester 40, or your own threads, with a shopping list for the threads you don't
  have.
- **Fidelity:** Standard (the default) or High. High allows more colours (up to 24; Photo Stitch 2
  keeps 4).
- **Photo Stitch 1:** finer rows, detail weighted by where the eye looks, dithering only in tonal
  areas, and local search on the result. A Trims choice gives fewer trims or the best likeness.
- **Photo Stitch 2:** colours chosen by appearance and mixed in linear light, within its needle
  limits, with no stitch under 1 mm.
- **Auto Punch:** sews the coloured background, works at 800 px with a 2 mm² region floor, keeps
  satin up to 2.5 mm, and sews thin parts as skeleton satin.
- **Cross Stitch:** each cell takes its mean colour; filled crosses by default, part-crosses as an
  option, 2.5 mm cells.
- **Design Center:** an automatic (Otsu) threshold, and bold lines sewn as parallel running lines.

### Under the hood

- Digitizing maths is now deterministic, and the same image gives the same stitches on Windows,
  macOS and Linux. Golden hashes check this in CI on all three systems.
- The editor's test suite runs in about a minute instead of 45.

## Before you sew

- **Do a test sew-out on scrap fabric** before using a design on anything that matters. No
  OpenStitch file has been sewn on a Brother machine as part of testing yet. The likeness scores
  compare renders, not sewn pieces; the calibration sew-out that ties them to real fabric is
  still to come.
- **OpenStitch mode is better on average, not on every image. Try both.**
  - Photo Stitch 1 is below the PE-DESIGN-compatible mode on the bright, high-key test photo,
    and it makes more trims (for example 904 against 385 on the portrait) unless you choose
    fewer trims.
  - Photo Stitch 2 is only slightly above the PE-DESIGN-compatible mode on average, and below it
    on some images. Its line look limits how close it can get.
  - The plan's targets (85 for Photo Stitch 1 at High fidelity, 95 for graphics) are not met
    yet, except by Design Center.
- **Photo Stitch 1 in the dialog:** Colour mode starts with Colours set to 1, so a colour photo
  comes out in two threads. Raise the Colours slider before converting
  ([#281](https://github.com/cristianonescu/OpenStitch/issues/281); also in alpha 8).
- **Thread charts:** brand colours come only from each maker's published chart. Brothread and
  Madeira Classic publish no colour values, so they are missing.
- **Other known differences from PE-DESIGN 11:** see "Known issues" in the README and
  [docs/COMPATIBILITY_AUDIT.md](../COMPATIBILITY_AUDIT.md). They are unchanged since alpha 8.
- **Keep your original PE-DESIGN files.** Save OpenStitch's output under a new name.
- **macOS:** double-clicking a file in Finder does not open it in OpenStitch yet.
