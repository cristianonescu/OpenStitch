# OpenStitch 0.1.0 alpha 13

The thirteenth alpha of OpenStitch, an independent, cross-platform embroidery design application
for macOS, Windows and Linux, built to work with Brother machines and with designs made in
PE-DESIGN 11. OpenStitch is not affiliated with Brother Industries.

Alpha 13 is the largest alpha so far, with about 240 issues closed. Its main additions:
- **Satin that you shape:** corner styles, short stitches, raised satin and satin looks. A branching
  shape becomes one satin column per arm.
- **Many new fills and lines:** colour blends, tartan, maze, wave and flow fills, stitch angle lines,
  and hand-embroidery lines.
- **Better image tools,** with fewer stitches piled in place and less travel sewn on top of finished
  thread.
- **More file formats.**
- **An editor that also runs in a web browser.**

Some defaults changed, so a design made the same way can sew differently; see "Changed output"
below. **It is still an alpha for testing, not for production work.** Read "Before you sew" below.

- **macOS:** the first time, right-click `OpenStitch.app` and choose **Open**, then **Open**
  again. Alternatively, run `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/OpenStitch.app`.

### Satin and branch columns

- **Satin you shape, beyond PE-DESIGN and off unless you choose it:**
  - corner styles (mitre or cap)
  - short stitches on the inside of curves
  - pull compensation per side and as a percentage
  - automatic and second underlay
  - satin looks (S-stitch, open zigzag, double, jagged edge)
  - raised (padded) satin

  They work on Manual Punch columns, zigzag lines, skeleton satin, Auto satin and satin text
  (#520–#523, #528, #531, #675, #676, #685, #686).
- **Satin line with a variable width:** set the width while you draw, drag width handles on the
  canvas, or import an SVG stroke as a satin line of the same width (#524, #700, #701, #707).
- **Edit Manual Punch rungs on the canvas:** add, delete and drag them, or swap sides (#525).
- **Convert a branching shape to satin:** one column per arm, sewn out to the shape's edges and
  joined into each other. Y, K, X, R, A, y, a ring and a five-point star meet the measured targets
  at 10 and 25 mm (#526 part). A star too broad for one column is split one column per point.
- **Sharp Corners and Miter Limit** on zigzag and satin lines, sewn as PE-DESIGN 11 sews them, and
  kept in PES 0110 files (#373).
- **Zigzag under sewing as PE-DESIGN sews it:** two runs at width/2 − 0.5 mm, or four runs from
  4 mm wide (#964).
- **Fixes:** a narrow Auto satin column no longer starts with a 39.5 mm stitch (#460), hairline
  satin sews as a running line (#368), covers no longer spike at a heart's cusps (#466), and a
  pointed start no longer ties in back and forth on its tip (#732).

### Fills and lines

- **New fills, beyond PE-DESIGN and off unless you choose them:**
  - colour blends and gradient fills, with a Gradient tool and gradients imported from SVG (#532)
  - tartan (#539)
  - maze, and meander with 12 tiles of its own (#533)
  - wave fill and flow fill (#538)
  - 14 named tatami textures (#537)
- **Fill Angle tool and stitch angle lines:** drag across regions to turn their rows, or draw
  lines that bend the rows inside one fill. The lines move with their shape (#534, #859, #890).
- **Fill along a direction curve** follows the curve. A U-shaped curve sews its inside without
  travelling over finished rows (#471, #923, #927, #947).
- **Push compensation** on fills, and a second underlay layer on fill and satin (#677).
- **Embossed and engraved stamps** as strong as PE-DESIGN's (#471, #956).
- **New lines:**
  - hand-made lines (back stitch, a thickness profile, a hand-stitch look, scribble)
  - ripple lines
  - decorative hand-embroidery lines
  - motif groups and motif bands along satin columns

  (#540–#544)
- **Fixed from the full-size review:**
  - the programmable fill's pattern shows (#459)
  - centre-out fills lose their seam (#461)
  - stippling keeps its spacing with no margin and works on narrow bands (#462, #638, #708)
  - stem, chain and candlewick lines are even on curves (#464)
  - the laurel and stars frames trim once or not at all, where they trimmed 28–31 times (#467)

### Lettering

- **Satin letters closer to PE-DESIGN's:**
  - X is one diagonal sewn through
  - N, W and Z sew in one run, and "NNN" at 6 mm goes from 9 trims to 3
  - A's apex, V's foot and M's middle run on as PE-DESIGN's do

  (#446 part)
- **Satin text takes the satin shaping,** the satin looks and raised satin (#676, #686, #782).
- **Fill text under a Text Transform** sews stroke by stroke, as PE-DESIGN 11.50 does (#428).
- **Fonts:** recommended size ranges and a size warning, OpenStitch Line for small text, a pixel
  font, and imported Ink/Stitch font folders (#527, #529, #530, #536).
- **Text envelopes** with handles you drag (#535), and **arched text in several lines** as rings
  round one centre (#456).
- **Monograms and small text:** Diamond and Circle monograms keep every stroke, and small
  lettering keeps the i's dot (#458, #465).
- **Trims and ties per object,** including trims between letters, words or lines (#548).

### Image tools: Auto Punch, Photo Stitch, Cross Stitch

- **Auto Punch, OpenStitch mode:**
  - **Branching strokes become satin columns,** one per arm. Painted and photo regions that only
    happen to branch stay one fill or satin (#702).
  - **Fills sew in the order that lays the least travel on finished thread,** with no more trims.
    The g01 emblem goes from 72 mm of travel on top to none (#469).
  - **A paper or cream background counts as the fabric** and is left out: the g08 lettering goes
    from 7,634 to 2,093 stitches (#445).
  - **Thin features are sewn as running lines.** A short one never costs a colour stop (#469).
  - **A small, distinct colour drawn as a solid patch keeps its own thread:** the balloon's basket
    is brown again (#962).
  - **No more knots of stitches in one spot** at specks, outlines, pointed tips and tie-offs
    (#688, #703, #732). Line art keeps its dots (#713).
- **Auto Punch, PE-compatible mode** keeps as many threads as colours, as PE-DESIGN does: the
  storybook picture sews 10 threads with the fox's orange, where it sewed 8 (#447).
- **Photo Stitch 1, PE-compatible mode:**
  - It travels between a colour's pieces instead of trimming, as PE-DESIGN does: trims went from
    1,667 to 120 across the test pictures (#298).
  - Dark thread no longer runs across light areas: on the portrait, the longest such stretch went
    from 7.8 to 4.5 mm (#915).
  - At High fidelity it's fast again (#433).
- **The image wizard** has a region table with reasons, click-to-fix, the colour order and a Preset
  row. It can cut out the subject in one click and export the outline as SVG, without an AI model
  (#489, #490).
- **Flow fill from the picture** (OpenStitch mode, off unless you choose it): fill rows follow fur,
  petals and hair (#538, #894).
- **Cross Stitch:** a Times setting as in PE-DESIGN, plus half, upright and Smyrna kinds (#614).

### Appliqué and production

- **Appliqué wizard:** the cover zigzag no longer sews under sewing, as PE-DESIGN's cover doesn't
  (#975). A choice sews the kept pattern last (#470).
- **Basting box, machine stops and Stop at Jumps** for machines without a thread cutter, and a
  **nearest start** that cuts travel by 10 % on average across the examples (#509, #512, #547).
- **Knockdown fill** under the design or the selection, for towels and fleece (#510).
- **Leave out stitching hidden under later objects,** travel under later fills, auto-route and
  Redwork; all off unless you choose them (#511, #545, #546).
- **Test swatches** of the selection, and a **fabric calibration card** (#452, #453, #507).
- **Fabrics and threads:**
  - 13 fabric presets with their makers' advice
  - thread weight changes the density
  - machine profiles and a first-run Machine Setup

  (#508)
- **Job sheets** for the client and the operator (#513), and **garment mockups** at true size on a
  T-shirt, cap or tote (#454).
- **Quality checks:** the Check Design pane, a density map, Review Sewing Order and an export
  read-back (#503–#506).

### Files

- **New formats:**
  - VP3, read and written with the thread brand, number and name (#480)
  - TAP, TBF and U01, read and written (#481)
  - DSB, DSZ, KSM, ZXY and STX, read (#481)
- **PES:**
  - versions 4, 5 and 7–10 are written as PE-DESIGN's Save As writes them (#374)
  - a file opened as PES 0110 exports as PES 0110 (#120, #398)
  - PE-DESIGN's texts and stitch groups import (#397)
- **CSD and SHV** export as PE-DESIGN exports them (#349 part).
- **Thread lists:** CSV, INF, EDR, COL and TXT, and GIMP palettes (#482, #483).
- **More exports:** a picture, a stitch-out video, a ZIP bundle, a stitch list, G-code and export

- **SVG import:** clip paths, masks, gradients as colour bands, and stitch settings taken from layer
  and object names (#487).
- **Large files open in the background,** with progress and Cancel (#558).
- **Design thumbnails** in Windows Explorer (with the MSI) and in macOS Quick Look (#557, #669).

  - threads match by perceived colour, and similar colours merge

- **Rulers and guides:** 0 is at the page centre, as in PE-DESIGN 11.50, and guides drag straight out

- **Editing tools:**
  - align and distribute, snap to objects, a precision bar with skew, and select same

  - copies along a line, scattered inside a shape, or as a kaleidoscope (#476)
  - the Knife (#477), Smooth Shape and Smooth Stitches (#478), and continuing a line from either
    end (#479)
  - frames, per-corner radii, spiral lines, gaps in an outline, Divide and the shape builder
    (#497)
- **Stitch Table** for exact needle-point edits (#501).
- **Working comfort:**
  - the Sewing Order list and the system clipboard (#500, #502)
  - autosave and recovery, and a command palette (#549, #550)
  - editable shortcuts, saved layouts and an accent colour (#551, #559)
  - designs in tabs (#552)
  - millimetres or inches (#519)
  - new-object defaults, Basic and Advanced attributes (#553, #656)
  - start cards, Getting Started and a Startup Wizard (#554, #657)

- **Views:** new view toggles, a zoom box and a display-calibrated real size (#517, #518). The

- **Hoops and sew-outs:** hoop lists for other brands, free multi-hooping and a stitch-out player

- **The editor runs in a web browser,** with file pickers and drag-and-drop; it remembers your

### The Assistant

## Changed output: the same design can sew differently

These defaults changed. Each item says how to get the old behaviour back where there is a way.

- **Running Stitch Path on open fills (#628):**
  - **What changed:** an open fill under 3 rows per mm now travels along its outline, where the
    open rows don't hide travel across them. On a 20 mm square at 2 rows/mm, the longest stretch
    across the rows went from 22.4 to 2.1 mm.
  - **Not affected:** fills at normal density, and PE-compatible output.
  - **Old behaviour:** set Running Stitch Path to **Inside**.
- **Auto Punch fill section order, OpenStitch mode (#469):**
  - **What changed:** a fill sews its sections in the order that lays the least travel on
    finished thread (`runningStitchPath: "insideHidden"`), never with more trims than Inside.
  - **Old behaviour:** set the region's Running Stitch Path to **Inside**.
- **Branching strokes in Auto Punch, OpenStitch mode (#702):**
  - **What changed:** they split into satin columns by default, but only long strokes whose columns
    cover them closely.
  - **Old behaviour:** untick "Split branching strokes (+)", or send `splitBranches: false`.
- **Blends and tartan trims (#954):**
  - **What changed:** a connector is never sewn where more than 2 mm of it would show; the thread
    trims instead. The gradients swatch goes from 58 to 162 trims.
  - **To reduce it:** there is no switch back; the rows or bands dither, or fewer colours, trims
    less.
- **Appliqué cover (#975):**
  - **What changed:** the wizard's cover zigzag has no under sewing.
  - **Old behaviour:** turn it on with the line's under sewing setting (`object.setLine`
    `underSewing`).
- **Zigzag under sewing (#964):** its runs move for every width except 2 mm, and lines from 4 mm
  wide get four runs. Patch borders change.
- **Zigzag corners (#373):** corners are cut only below 67.5°, where before they were cut below
  90°. Satin lines' sharp corners follow the same rule.
- **Threads for image tools (#810, #820):**
  - **What changed:** an image conversion that names no threads uses your default chart, now New
    Brothread.
  - **Old behaviour:** choose Brother's chart in the wizard (`threads: "brother"`), or make it
    your default.
- **Auto Punch, PE-compatible mode (#447):** a picture keeps as many threads as colours. The
  storybook sews 10 threads where it sewed 8.
- **Photo Stitch 1, PE-compatible mode (#298, #915):**
  - **What changed:** it travels in sight instead of trimming, with no more than 8 mm showing and
    no dark thread over light.
  - **In the kit:** the portrait's trims go from 13 to 38, and the sunset's from 14 to 27.
- **Auto Punch, OpenStitch mode (#445, #688, #460, #469, #962):**
  - a paper background is left out
  - specks are left out (an override can still sew one)
  - a region whose satin would cross 12–16 mm is filled
  - thin features become running lines
  - a small distinct colour keeps its thread
- **Ties (#703, #732):** at most 6 stitches in place at a tie-in or tie-off.
- **Lettering:**
  - N, W and Z lose trims, and corners run on at N, M, A and V (#446)
  - fill text under a Text Transform sews by stroke (#428)
  - arched letters sit 0.05 mm closer (#456)
- **Fills:**
  - travel walks the outline round a tip
  - angle lines and gradient axes move with their shape
  - fills along a U-shaped curve sew its inside
  - stamps are stronger
  - stippling, centre-out fills, cross stitch and zigzag net on the page axes

  (#471, #534, #461, #462, #463)
- **Editor defaults, no change to stitches:**
  - guides are on, and rulers count from the page centre (Top-left is a setting)
  - a click inside a shape that sews no region no longer selects it
  - a file opened as PES 0110 exports as PES 0110

**Re-export designs** made with alpha 12 or earlier if you want the new stitching. A design file
keeps its own settings, and the stitches are made from them when it's exported.

## Before you sew

- **Do a test sew-out on scrap fabric** before using a design on anything that matters.
- **Known issues:**
  - **Branch columns:** N, M and W converted to satin get one column per stroke (coverage
    0.97–0.99), but M at 25 mm leaves 1.8 mm of outline bare by a join and W at 25 mm joins
    only 0.19 mm deep. Render them and keep one satin or a fill if they look thin (#526).
    <!-- TO FILL AT THE GATE: #526 N/M/W, done or still open. -->
  - **Zigzag on a closed outline** spaces its crossings along the centre line; PE-DESIGN spaces
    them by the outer edge's length (#978).
    <!-- TO FILL AT THE GATE: #978, done or still open. -->
  - **The appliqué wizard's tack-down** defaults to zigzag with a V every 3 mm (151 stitches on the
    a01 reference). PE-DESIGN sews a V every 5 mm (98) (#982).
    <!-- TO FILL AT THE GATE: #982, done or still open. -->
  - **Auto Punch and Cross Stitch from pictures:** edge cells can become a halo thread, and the
    cross grid doesn't snap to pixel art (#469).
  - **SHV export** settles its preview, palette fill and long stitches from one PE-DESIGN reference
    only (#349).
  - **Dithered colour blends** cost many trims (#951).
  - **The browser editor** has no Assistant yet (#766).
  - Other known differences from PE-DESIGN 11 are in the README's "Known issues" and
    [docs/COMPATIBILITY_AUDIT.md](../COMPATIBILITY_AUDIT.md).
- **Keep your original PE-DESIGN files.** Save OpenStitch's output under a new name.
- **macOS:** double-clicking a file in Finder doesn't open it in OpenStitch yet; use File ▸ Open.
